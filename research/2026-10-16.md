## @applied_stoic slot 1: SWAP. Musonius Rufus -> Seneca Letter 28: travel won't cure restlessness
Why the swap: there's no public-domain translation of Musonius Rufus by an approved translator (PLAYBOOK allows Long, Gummere, Basore; the standard English Musonius is Cora Lutz, 1947). This topic is in the backlog.
Source: https://monadnock.net/seneca/28.html (Letter 28, "On Travel as a Cure for Discontent", tr. Richard Mott Gummere). Cite "Seneca · Letter 28 · tr. Richard M. Gummere".
Verbatim (each checked against the page Oct 12):
- "Do you suppose that you alone have had this experience? Are you surprised, as if it were a novelty, that after such long travel and so many changes of scene you have not been able to shake off the gloom and heaviness of your mind? You need a change of soul rather than a change of climate."
- "Why do you wonder that globe-trotting does not help you, seeing that you always take yourself with you?" (Seneca is quoting Socrates' answer to someone who complained of this; say "Seneca, quoting Socrates" if you use it.)
- "... your faults will follow you whithersoever you travel." (the start of that sentence, verbatim: "Though you may cross vast spaces of sea, and though, as our Vergil remarks," — the middle wasn't captured, so quote only the end with a leading ellipsis, or the whole first part plus "...")
- "The knowledge of sin is the beginning of salvation." (Seneca: "This saying of Epicurus seems to me to be a noble one." — say "Seneca, quoting Epicurus")
- Context you may state: Lucilius had traveled a lot and still felt gloomy; Seneca says the problem travels with him.
- Modern angle: the vacation that didn't fix burnout, the move to a new city, the new job that felt the same by month three. Exercise: before the next escape, name the thing you're trying to leave behind.
- Don't invent travel statistics.
