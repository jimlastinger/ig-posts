## @applied_stoic slot 1: Marcus's Book 1 gratitude list; write yours
Source: https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.1.one.html (Marcus Aurelius, Meditations Book 1, tr. George Long)
The page doesn't number sections; numbers below follow the standard paragraph order (1.1, 1.2 ...).

- 1.1: "From my grandfather Verus I learned good morals and the government of my temper."
- 1.2: "From the reputation and remembrance of my father, modesty and a manly character." (page prints "amanly", a typo for "a manly")
- 1.3: "From my mother, piety and beneficence, and abstinence, not only from evil deeds, but even from evil thoughts; and further, simplicity in my way of living, far removed from the habits of the rich."
- 1.4: "From my great-grandfather, not to have frequented public schools, and to have had good teachers at home, and to know that on such things a man should spend liberally."
- 1.5: "From my governor, to be neither of the green nor of the blue party at the games in the Circus, nor a partizan either of the Parmularius or the Scutarius at the gladiators' fights; from him too I learned endurance of labour, and to want little, and to work with my own hands, and not to meddle with other people's affairs, and not to be ready to listen to slander."
  (Modern angle: his tutor taught him not to be a sports-team partisan.)
- 1.7 (excerpts): "From Rusticus I received the impression that my character required improvement and discipline;" ... "and to read carefully, and not to be satisfied with a superficial understanding of a book;" ... "and I am indebted to him for being acquainted with the discourses of Epictetus, which he communicated to me out of his own collection."
- 1.17 opening: "To the gods I am indebted for having good grandfathers, good parents, a good sister, good teachers, good associates, good kinsmen and friends, nearly everything good."
- Context already used in earlier posts: Marcus ruled Rome AD 161 to 180; Meditations was a private notebook. Book 1 is a list of what he learned from specific people.
- Exercise idea: write "From ___, I learned ___" for three people.
